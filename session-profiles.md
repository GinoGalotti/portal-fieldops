# Session Profiles — Parameter Reference

How past sessions map to the tunable parameters. Use these as calibration when designing new sessions.

---

## S01 — A Promise Is a Promise (The Eszter Case)
**Parameters:** `low action / linear mystery / moderate NPCs (5) / dread tone / absent MESA / standard length`
**One sentence:** A dying woman's locket contains something that shouldn't exist, and a man who loved her is still alive because of it.
**What worked:** Slow build, emotional payoff, the team's first field deployment. Bálint as a single sympathetic NPC anchor.
**What didn't:** Players were cautious — first session energy. Some pacing drag in the middle.
**NPC count:** 5 (Bálint, Eszter's memory, lab staff in briefing, one neighbour, one witness)
**Lesson:** Linear mystery works when the emotional stakes are clear from the start.

## S02 — Something That Wants to Be Known (Aldermoor / The Cartographer)
**Parameters:** `low action / layered mystery / moderate NPCs (6) / dread tone / background MESA / standard length`
**One sentence:** A neighbourhood is losing its sense of place, and the entity responsible doesn't know it's doing anything wrong.
**What worked:** The Cartographer as a sympathetic non-malicious entity. Alan's art installation. Diane Marsh's notebook. The entity resolution through understanding rather than force.
**What didn't:** The MESA estate office discovery felt disconnected from the main case. Optional Rook confrontation was good but could have been integrated better.
**NPC count:** 6 (Arthur Okafor, Diane Marsh, Vera Osei, PC Adeyemi, Margaret Holt's house, Rook optional)
**Lesson:** Background MESA works when players find evidence naturally. The estate office was a location, not a scene with NPCs — that's the right density for a background thread.

## S03 — The Understudies (Meridian Theatre)
**Parameters:** `medium action / branching mystery / dense NPCs (12+) / uncanny tone / confrontation MESA / two-parter (3.5h + 2h)`
**One sentence:** Two dead actors are performing through their understudies, and the mechanism that holds them is the performance itself.
**What worked:** Clara and Edmund's emotional resonance. The Outlaws read-through (comedy highlight). Nadia's blogpost as a prophetic nudge. Alan writing "Clara, exit stage left" — unplanned, player-driven, perfect. Alan jumping on stage and getting possessed by Proteus — chaotic, dramatic, players going all-in on playbook abilities. Edmund's farewell during the standing ovation. Splitting the party for Part 2's hospital scenes. Meeting Veritas/Rook (Sven's player: "extremely exciting"). CAMPBELL suspicion building organically.
**What didn't:** Too many NPCs — even the keeper got confused mid-session. The casting chain (Clara→Miriam→Sophie, Edmund→Daniel→James) was too many names at once. Route A/B/C felt gamey in Part 1. Investigation had no exit conditions — players didn't know when they'd found "enough." Reed felt useless in supernatural scenes; Rex had nothing non-supernatural to do during the hospital. The fey breadcrumbs were "frustrating confusing" not "intriguing confusing." Prompt book wasn't on stage (Reed's backpack) — partial resolution by design, but traces remain.
**NPC count:** 12+ (Daniel/Edmund, Miriam, Tom, Sophie, Petra, Marcus Yuen, James, Constance, Leo, Nina, Amir, Rook, grunts)
**CRITICAL FEEDBACK:** "Too many characters. Confusing. Wanted more action and a clearer monster."

**Actual play observations (Part 1 — ~3.5 hours):**
- Players went to the theatre as a group — the branching structure collapsed into a linear path. Nobody split up.
- The casting chain was too many names at once. The keeper got confused mid-session.
- The Outlaws read-through was the session highlight — comedy, engagement, everyone involved. Feed line cards worked.
- **Alan shapeshifted into Sophie** (Changeling) — the session's most dramatic player-driven moment. Drove the entire MESA encounter. Sven walked through walls. When players commit to bold playbook moves, lean into them.
- **The team stole a MESA BIM collection array.** MESA knows. Consequences pending.
- Reed's player explicitly said "I feel useless" during supernatural investigation scenes.
- Investigation expanded to fill available time — no clear exit conditions at each clue source.
- CAMPBELL suspicion building naturally ("in Claude I trust, in Campbell I am not sure").
- Players wanted clearer direction, not more open-endedness.

**Actual play observations (Part 2 — ~2 hours):**
- **Splitting the party worked** — hospital gave momentum; each sub-team had a clear scene.
- **Alan's fey monologue** (describing the fey afterlife to Miriam/Clara) cost a fey point and made Sven see it too. Unscripted. Perfect. Vision implantation is now confirmed player canon.
- **"Clara, exit stage left"** — Alan wrote a script as a prop. Clara read it, chose to leave. Jar emptied. The keeper let this work because the role was good. Trust the players when they find creative resolutions within the fiction's logic.
- **Alan jumping on stage** and getting possessed by Proteus — chaotic, got a laugh, drove the climax. The keeper confirmed: bold stupid moves are what the game is for.
- **Reed forced the standing ovation** (not Sven, as designed). The players found their own resolution vectors. The ovation built from Reed → Rex → the audience. Edmund said "There is peace on the other side. Thank you." and departed.
- The prompt book was NOT on stage (Reed's backpack) — partial resolution. Traces remain in the theatre. This was the planned consequence and it landed correctly.
- **"Fake it till you make it"** — players defined the prompt book's mechanism as a therapeutic science direction. Player-defined theory, now lab canon.
- The CAMPBELL integrity test (planting false info) was player-initiated and unprompted. They're testing the system, not just following it.
- Part 2 was tighter and more comprehensible than Part 1 (Rex's player said so directly). Focused scenes > open investigation.
- Total runtime: ~5.5 hours across two sessions. The investigation was too long. The resolution scenes (hospital + performance) were the right length.

**Lesson:** Dense NPCs only work if the social web IS the mystery. For a theatre case the ensemble was thematic but exceeded player capacity. Cut supporting cast to named-when-encountered. Route labels are keeper architecture — hide them from the table. Investigation needs exit conditions: each clue source ends with "you now know X, which points you to Y." Split-party scenes work when each sub-team has a clear dramatic purpose. The climax was player-shaped — the keeper had designed "Sven onstage," the players delivered "Reed's ovation + Alan possessed + Edmund's farewell." The outcome was better. Design the destination, leave the path open.

## S04 — The Shōjō (Baltimore Docks) — PLANNED
**Parameters:** `high action / linear mystery / minimal NPCs (4) / action tone / aftermath MESA / short-standard length`
**One sentence:** A drunk sea god is killing people at the Baltimore docks, and the team has one night to stop it before MESA exploits their absence.
**Design response to S03 feedback:**
- Half the NPCs (4 vs 12+), no casting chains, no web of relationships to track
- Linear path forward — briefing sends them to the docks, NPCs point them toward the creature, weakness discovery enables the fight
- Real combat with a real creature — three escalating encounters, actual harm, a stat block players can engage with
- **Reed:** civilian management during the emotional distortion field is explicitly his. He's the one keeping bystanders safe.
- **Rex:** first gadget-focused combat. His scanner and improvised weapons are the party's damage source. Non-supernatural levers designed in.
- **Alan:** glamour works on the detective but NOT on the Shōjō (it sees through pretence). Useful for investigation, humbled in the fight. Good arc beat.
- **Sven:** the Shōjō's reaction to draining a ghost is the session's emotional core. Bold Monstrous moves are designed for.
- Investigation has clear exit conditions: Mariana gives the sighting → Pulaski gives the coroner data → Yuki explains the folklore → Rex scans the cask. Each discovery points to the next.
- MESA involvement is aftermath only — no MESA NPCs in the active session, consequences discovered on the drive home.
**NPC count:** 4 (Yuki Tanaka, Detective Pulaski, Mariana Costa, the Shōjō)
**Lesson (projected):** The palate cleanser session should feel categorically different from what preceded it.

## Parameter Presets

Quick-start profiles for common session types:

### "The Investigation"
`low-medium action / layered mystery / moderate NPCs / dread-uncanny tone`
Best for: cases where understanding IS the resolution. Entity cases, mystery cases.

### "The Monster Hunt"  
`high action / linear mystery / minimal NPCs / action tone`
Best for: palate cleansers, creature-focused sessions, when players want to hit something.

### "The Heist"
`medium action / linear mystery / moderate NPCs / action tone`
Best for: MESA facility infiltration, time-pressure scenarios, extraction missions.

### "The Emotional Gut-Punch"
`low action / layered mystery / moderate NPCs / melancholy tone`
Best for: character arc resolutions, NPC death sessions, campaign turning points.

### "The Comedy Episode"
`medium action / linear mystery / minimal NPCs / comic tone`
Best for: one-offs, palate cleansers, April Fools, sessions after heavy arc episodes.
