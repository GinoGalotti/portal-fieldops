# S04 — The Shōjō (Working Outline)

**Status:** Draft for review. Not final session prep.
**Tone:** Action-forward. One creature, one location, one night. The comedy is in the situation, not the NPCs. The dread is underneath — MESA is moving while you're away.
**Player feedback addressed:** Linear structure, fewer characters, clear monster, real combat, less to remember.

---

## The Setup

Emergency override. CAMPBELL flags three deaths near the Baltimore Inner Harbor — no common cause, no common demographic, all within 400 metres of the waterfront in the past five days. The only shared factor: witnesses describe each victim as being in an unusually intense emotional state immediately before death.

PORTAL can't wait. Civilians are dying. The hunters drive to Baltimore — two hours from the lab, deployed Friday afternoon, expected back Saturday morning.

They will not be back Saturday morning.

---

## What the Hunters Know (Briefing)

- Three deaths, five days, Baltimore Inner Harbor
- Victim 1: Man celebrating a job promotion at a waterfront bar. Died laughing. Cardiac arrest. Expression of absolute happiness frozen on his face.
- Victim 2: Woman sitting alone on a bench overlooking the harbour. Found peaceful, no cause of death identified. She'd been visiting the spot where she scattered her mother's ashes.
- Victim 3: Dockworker, found dead in a loading bay. Witnesses say he'd been in a screaming argument with his supervisor — a fight that was also, weirdly, a reconciliation. Both men were crying. Then one of them stopped.
- Police have no theory. The coroner has no theory. The deaths are unconnected by age, background, or circumstance.
- CAMPBELL's assessment: BIM-frequency spike detected in the area. Consistent with entity presence. No visual confirmation. No pattern match in the database.
- Director's note: "This is close to home. Resolve it fast and get back. I need you here this weekend."

**What they don't know:** What the creature is, why it's here, what it feeds on, or how to stop it.

---

## NPCs (four total)

**Yuki Tanaka** — Event coordinator for Hosokawa Shipping's cultural exchange programme. 34. Organised the sake tastings and the floating restaurant on the converted barge. She knows what a Shōjō is — her grandmother told her stories. She doesn't believe they're real until the hunters describe what's happening, at which point she goes very quiet. She can explain the folklore, identify the ceremonial cask, and tell them about the brewer's daughter who died. She is practical, shaken, and helpful.

**Detective Ray Pulaski** — Baltimore PD homicide. 51. Three unconnectable deaths in his jurisdiction and his captain wants answers. He doesn't want PORTAL here — he doesn't know what PORTAL is and he doesn't care. He wants credentials or he wants them gone. Alan's glamour works on him. Reed's sincerity works better. He has the witness statements and the coroner reports and will share them if the hunters are straight with him. He won't be at the final fight — he's a daytime investigator, not a dockyard-at-2am person.

**Mariana Costa** — Dockworker. 28. She was working the night shift when the third victim died. She saw something. She's been telling people and nobody believes her: "a very tall man with red hair, standing in the fog between the containers, and he was — I know how this sounds — he was singing." She's scared and angry and wants someone to take her seriously. She can lead the hunters to where the Shōjō has been spotted.

**The Shōjō itself** — Not an NPC in the conversational sense, but it communicates. Emotional impressions, fragments of song, the occasional slurred word in archaic Japanese. It is grieving. It has been grieving for a long time. It doesn't understand what it's doing to people. It's just so thirsty.

That's it. Four characters. One of them is the monster.

## Session Structure — Linear

### Hook: CAMPBELL Emergency Briefing
Fast. The deaths are close to the lab. CAMPBELL flags it as imminent — proximity to civilians, escalating pattern. The Director overrides whatever the hunters were planning. "This isn't a queue pick. Go now."

The drive to Baltimore is two hours. Use it: the hunters talk in the van, review the case file, argue about approach. If anyone tries to call the lab during the case, cell coverage near the waterfront is patchy (2005 — no smartphones, spotty service around the harbour industrial zone). They're on their own.

### Scene 1: The Docks by Day — Investigation

The hunters arrive late afternoon. The waterfront is active — dockworkers, a few tourist spots, the Hosokawa Shipping barge visible at the quay. Three crime scenes, one active detective, and a dockworker who saw something nobody believes.

**What investigation reveals (in any order — not routes, just what's findable):**

*The deaths:* The witness statements and coroner reports (from Pulaski or from the scenes themselves) reveal the emotional intensity pattern. Rex can notice: three different causes of death, but all three victims showed neurological markers consistent with extreme emotional overstimulation. The brain scans look like the victims felt something so intensely that their nervous systems simply stopped. This is not a poison. This is not a weapon. Something drained them through their feelings.

*The creature:* Mariana Costa's sighting. "Tall, red-haired, singing." She saw it between the containers near the loading bay where victim 3 died. She also noticed: the fog around it smelled like alcohol. Sweet, rice-based. Not beer, not whisky. Sake.

*The source:* The Hosokawa barge. The cultural exchange programme. The sake tastings. Yuki Tanaka, when the hunters find her, explains: one of the ceremonial casks they shipped from Japan was special. It was brewed by a master brewer whose daughter had just died. The grief was in the liquid — Yuki's grandmother would have said the cask was cursed, but Yuki doesn't use that word. She uses the word "heavy." In Japanese folklore, a Shōjō is a sea spirit that drinks. What it drinks depends on what's available. If the sake contains real sorrow, the Shōjō drinks that. And then it wants more. And then it drinks from the nearest source.

*The BIM reading:* Rex's scanner (whatever he built in S03) reads the barge and the cask. The cask reads elevated — not as high as the prompt book, but significant. The barge and the surrounding docks read ambient-elevated in a radius. The signature is different from the Aldermoor/Eszter class — this is something older, something that doesn't match MESA's frequency. This isn't engineered. This is natural. First time they've encountered that.

**The mystery clicks when:** The hunters connect the emotional intensity of the deaths to the creature's feeding pattern AND trace it back to the ceremonial cask. The deaths aren't random — the Shōjō is drawn to whoever is feeling the most intensely, closest to the waterfront. It drinks their emotion until there's nothing left. The cask is both what summoned it and what can contain it. Yuki and the folklore point to the weakness.

### Scene 2: First Encounter — Dusk

As evening falls, the emotional distortion field activates. The Shōjō is waking up.

**The field:** People near the waterfront start behaving strangely. A couple on a date are suddenly, overwhelmingly in love — not sweetly, dangerously. A fisherman who was mildly annoyed about his catch is now in a towering rage, throwing equipment. A street performer who was happy is now ecstatic, manic, dancing so hard she's going to hurt herself.

The hunters have to manage this. These aren't enemies — they're civilians being emotionally amplified. Reed's territory. Act Under Pressure to calm people down, Protect Someone to get the manic dancer away from the quay edge. Every civilian they stabilise, another one pops. The distortion is spreading.

**Then they see it.**

Between the containers, in the harbour fog, something enormous moves. Red-haired, ten feet tall, swaying. Singing something that makes the air heavier. It's beautiful and wrong.

If they engage: it is surprised, mildly annoyed, and enormously strong. First hit bounces off (armour 2). It swats the nearest hunter away (3-harm close) with the casualness of someone brushing away a fly. It doesn't want to fight. It wants to drink. It goes back to feeding.

If they try to talk: it regards them with bleary, ancient warmth. It doesn't fully understand English. It communicates in emotional impressions — they feel what it feels. It is lonely. It is thirsty. It found something that tasted like real sorrow and it can't stop.

**The Share the Cup move fires** if any hunter stays close too long. Everyone nearby rolls +Cool.
- 10+: You feel its grief but hold yourself. You understand it.
- 7-9: The sorrow is overwhelming. Take -1 forward.
- Miss: You're inside it. 2-harm (ignore-armour, weird). Can't act against it until someone pulls you out.

**The encounter ends** when the hunters retreat, the Shōjō wanders off following the strongest emotion in the area, or someone gets hurt badly enough to force a withdrawal. The hunters have learned: this thing is too strong to fight unprepared, it feeds on emotions, and it's not deliberately cruel. Now they need the weakness.

### Investigation Beat: Finding the Weakness

Between encounters, probably 30-60 minutes of play. The hunters regroup and work the problem.

**What they need to find:**
1. The ceremonial cask is the anchor — the Shōjō is bound to it
2. The sake itself is the weapon — ceremonial sake from the grief-cask can hurt it, contain it, or satisfy it
3. Understanding what it is (from Yuki's folklore knowledge) makes it legible — armour drops from 2 to 1

**How they find it:**
- Rex examines the cask properly. The BIM reading shows a two-way connection — the creature and the cask are linked. Disrupting one affects the other.
- Yuki explains the folklore: Shōjō can be appeased with an offering of exceptional sake. If the sake is given freely and with genuine feeling, the spirit drinks its fill and returns to the sea. But the offering has to be real — performed, not faked. The Shōjō sees through pretence.
- The cask itself: still half-full. The ceremonial sake inside it is charged. Rex can confirm it reads as BIM-active. This is their ammunition AND their containment tool.

**Tactical preparation (let the players plan):**
- The sake can be used as a weapon (applied to Rex's gadgets, thrown, used to coat weapons — +1 harm against the Shōjō)
- The sake can be poured in a circle to create a containment boundary (the creature can't cross it while the circle holds — but the circle breaks if anyone inside it feels nothing)
- The sake can be offered as a drink — the diplomacy path. Someone walks up to a ten-foot grief-drunk sea spirit and shares a cup with genuine emotion. Roll +Weird. This is the Drink With the Spirit custom move.

**The drunk question:** Someone at the table will ask "what if we get drunk?" Let them. The +1 to investigation / -1 to coordination / +1 harm from Emotional Drain tradeoff is real. It should be a choice, not a strategy. One or two hunters drunk maximum — the whole team drunk is a party wipe.

### Scene 3: The Real Fight — Night

The Shōjō is feeding more aggressively now. The emotional distortion field has spread across the waterfront. Civilians are in danger. Time pressure: by morning, the radius will reach the residential blocks.

**The fight has a shape:**

*Phase 1 — Approach:* The hunters need to get to the Shōjō through the distortion field. Civilians in the way. The couple from earlier are now physically fighting. The fisherman has a knife. The street performer is dancing on the edge of the quay. Reed and Alan handle the civilians. Rex and Sven push toward the creature.

*Phase 2 — Engagement:* Armour is 1 now (they understand it). Rex's sake-enhanced gadgets can deal real damage. The creature fights back — Staggering Blow (3-harm close), Emotional Drain (2-harm ignore-armour), The Singing (soft move — draws civilians toward it, hunters must Act Under Pressure to resist). The Shōjō is not tactical — it's drunk and grieving and enormous. Its attacks are wide, clumsy, devastatingly powerful. It's a barroom brawl with a sea god.

*Phase 3 — Resolution choice:* When the Shōjō is weakened (half harm capacity), it stops fighting and starts singing in earnest. The song is heartbreaking. This is the decision point:

**Fight path:** Keep hitting it. Drive it into the sake circle. The containment holds if the hunters maintain it — someone must stay in the circle and keep feeling something real. The Shōjō rages, then slumps, then is quiet. It dissolves into the cask. The cask seals. Done. The creature is contained, not killed — it can be dealt with properly later (or become someone else's problem).

**Diplomacy path:** Someone shares a cup with it. The Drink With the Spirit custom move. This requires walking up to a wounded, frightened, ten-foot sea spirit and offering it sake with genuine emotion — not performed emotion, real emotion. The Shōjō will know the difference. If it works: the creature drinks, sighs, and returns to the harbour. The cask goes cold. The distortion field collapses. The creature is gone — not contained, released. It chose to leave. The difference matters for how it's classified in the field report.

**The Sven moment:** During the fight, the Shōjō tries to drain Sven. What happens next is the session's emotional reveal. The creature reacts to whatever it finds in him. This should not be scripted — ask Sven's player what Sven is feeling in this moment, and let the Shōjō's reaction reflect that.

## The Drive Home — What MESA Did

The hunters resolve the case Saturday night or early Sunday morning. They're tired, possibly hurt, two hours from base. They drive back.

When they arrive, one of these is waiting for them (pick based on which thread you want to advance):

**Option A — Gordon Avery:** A secondary PORTAL team was dispatched to Gordon's house three days ago. They arrived to find MESA already there — not helping Gordon, studying him. The secondary team was professionally and legally outmanoeuvred. MESA has Gordon's medical data, his proximity-drain readings, and the names of everyone he's been in contact with. The secondary team is still at the house. They need backup. Gordon's grandchildren are fine — barely. This is urgent.

**Option B — Nadia's Window:** Nadia Osei's consciousness tether has weakened dramatically. CAMPBELL detected it but couldn't reach the field team (cell coverage). Nadia's blog went dark twelve hours ago. When the hunters try to contact her, the connection is different — weaker, more fragmented. Her window is closing. MESA may have already reached her. If the hunters want to do Nadia's debrief before the tether fails, they have days, not weeks.

**Option C — Both, staggered:** Gordon's situation is the immediate crisis (people are in the house). Nadia's window is the ticking clock underneath. The hunters have to triage.

The point: while you were saving lives in Baltimore, the world kept moving. The emergency override wasn't a coincidence — MESA modelled PORTAL's deployment patterns. They knew the field team would be gone. The question of whether MESA engineered the Shōjō's emergence or merely exploited the timing should stay ambiguous. Rook would say: "We didn't make the creature appear. We just knew you'd have to deal with it."

- **Palate cleanser:** First real combat. First non-consciousness-transfer entity. First case with no MESA involvement in the mechanism itself.
- **Rex:** First gadget-focused combat. His scanner and his inventiveness are the party's damage source.
- **Reed:** Civilian management is his superpower. The distortion field scenes are his.
- **Alan:** Glamour works on the detective but NOT on the Shōjō (it sees through pretence). Useful for investigation, humbled in the fight. Good arc beat.
- **Sven:** The Shōjō's reaction to him is the session's emotional core. His nature matters in a new way.
- **MESA advancement:** Gordon and/or Nadia's threads advance without player input — consequences of being pulled away. Sets up the next session's stakes.
- **Tone variety:** This is Episode 4 of 9 in the arc. It should be the session players remember as "the one where we fought a drunk sea god at the Baltimore docks."

## How This Responds to S03 Feedback

Every design choice here addresses something that didn't work in S03:

| S03 Problem | S04 Response |
|-------------|-------------|
| Too many NPCs (12+), keeper and players both confused | 4 NPCs. One is the monster. |
| Branching routes invisible to players, group didn't split | Linear: briefing → docks → investigate → fight. One path forward. |
| Investigation had no exit conditions, expanded to 90 min | Each NPC gives one clue that points to the next step. Mariana → creature sighting. Pulaski → emotional intensity pattern. Yuki → folklore + weakness. Rex → cask BIM reading. Four discoveries, each with a clear "now you know X." |
| Reed felt useless during supernatural scenes | The distortion field is Reed's scene. Civilians need protecting. His empathy and competence are the right tools. |
| Rex had no non-supernatural levers in investigation | Rex's scanner, Rex's gadgets, Rex's analysis of the coroner data. Every scene has something Rex can examine, build, or figure out. |
| Fey breadcrumbs were "frustrating confusing" | No fey thread this session. Alan's glamour works on humans, fails on the creature — a clean, legible beat. |
| Players wanted more guidance | CAMPBELL's emergency override sends them. Each NPC points to the next. The creature appears at dusk whether they're ready or not. The session has momentum, not a menu. |
| No combat in three sessions | Three escalating encounters. A stat block. Actual harm. The drunk mechanic as a genuine tactical dilemma. |
| Bold player moves (Alan's shapeshifting, Sven through walls) were the highlight | Sven's interaction with the Shōjō is designed as an open moment. Alan's glamour has a clear legible limitation (the creature sees through it). Both players have designed space for bold moves. |
