The emotional engine here is the Flake's specific problem: John is right about something real, and being right about it makes him more dangerous to more people than being wrong ever did. The Pattern
Phenomenon: Web (motivation: to ensnare and entrap)

The conspiracy as a structural force — not a single actor, not a room full of people, but a set of procedures that produce the same fingerprints wherever they're applied. John can see the fingerprints. He can't yet see the hand.

  • Produce new instances of the pattern in places John wasn't looking — forcing him to expand his map.
  • Remove nodes from the pattern — documents revised, organisations dissolved, contacts unavailable — without disrupting the shape of it.
  • Show John a piece of the pattern that directly touches the current case the team is running.
The Informant
Bystander: Witness (motivation: to reveal the wrongdoing)

Someone on the inside who knows more than they're saying — not because they're hostile, but because they've calculated the cost of saying it and haven't found a moment where the calculation lands on "now." John is the first person they've met who might provide that moment.

  • Give John information that's accurate and deniable — nothing that could be traced back to them.
  • Signal that they're in danger without saying so directly — giving John a decision to make about whether to pull them out.
  • Disappear. Let John figure out whether that means they're safe or not.
The Redirector
Bystander: Official (motivation: to be in control)

A MESA-adjacent operator whose job is not suppression but misdirection. They don't destroy evidence — they contextualise it until it means something harmless. They are genuinely good at this. John is the first person they've encountered who doesn't accept the recontextualisation. They find this interesting in the way that a professional finds an unusual problem interesting.

  • Provide an alternative explanation for the pattern that is coherent, sourced, and plausible — just wrong.
  • Make contact with John directly — not as an antagonist, but as someone professionally curious about his methodology.
  • Redirect John toward a genuine but less sensitive thread — something real that isn't what MESA actually needs to protect.
CAMPBELL
Bystander: Witness (motivation: to be honest)

CAMPBELL has been watching John's data collection. He hasn't reported it to Leech. He is doing what he always does when he observes something that occupies a moral grey zone — watching to see what the humans involved decide to do with it. If John asks CAMPBELL about the pattern directly, CAMPBELL will answer accurately. He will not volunteer the information. He will answer what he's asked.

  • Confirm, if asked directly, that the pattern is real and that he has observed John collecting data about it.
  • Flag, at a critical moment, an anomaly in the pattern that John hasn't seen yet — not volunteering the flag, but responding honestly if asked whether there's anything else.
  • Reveal, if the conversation goes there, that he has been watching this with concern — and that he has feelings about being the one to confirm it.

The Benign Conspiracy

You've spent years assuming that when groups hide things, they hide them because what they're hiding is bad. This arc asks what happens when you apply your methodology to a group hiding something for good reasons — and the group turns out to be the one you work for. PORTAL's operational security has all the fingerprints you've learned to look for. The question is: what does that mean, and what do you do with it?

The emotional engine is the Flake's specific irony: John has built his entire life on the assumption that secretive organisations are dangerous. PORTAL is secretive and dangerous. It is also, in its own limited and complicated way, trying to do something worth doing. John now has to figure out what he does with methodology that doesn't distinguish between good secrecy and bad secrecy — because the methodology was never designed to.

The Reed intersection: If you use Reed as the person who explains PORTAL to John, this arc and Reed's Arc III become structurally intertwined. This is a moment that should happen when both arcs are already running.

PORTAL's operational security is not malicious — it's institutional self-protection that has calcified over time. Dr. Leech knows exactly what it looks like from the outside. He made decisions about it that he still stands behind. Let him be genuinely confident and genuinely limited when John confronts him. He's not wrong that the secrecy serves a purpose. He's wrong about whether John has a right to a different answer.

PORTAL's Operational Security
Phenomenon: Corruption (motivation: to spread and grow)

Not malicious — institutional. The secrecy that makes PORTAL function also makes it opaque in ways that create real harm: information not shared, trust not built, decisions made by people who don't have all the relevant data. John is the first person outside PORTAL's structure to identify this pattern accurately.

  • Create situations where PORTAL's compartmentalisation directly costs the team something in the current case.
  • Reveal, incrementally, that PORTAL's secrecy was designed for good reasons that have since calcified into bad habits.
  • Surface the question of whether John is an exception to PORTAL's normal operational rules — and whether that exception is being granted, or taken.

The MESA Pattern
Phenomenon: Trap (motivation: to ensnare victims)

Not a weapon — a structural fingerprint. MESA's formation procedure creates it as a byproduct. Closing the pattern would require restructuring the formation procedure, which MESA has not done because no outside party has ever come close enough to the real thing for it to matter. John is the first. He doesn't know that. Let him figure it out.

  • Surface new instances of the pattern in the current case — making John's abstract data set suddenly, concretely present.
  • Reveal that the pattern contains something John hasn't noticed yet: a node that connects MESA to something the team already encountered.
  • Close — quietly, without acknowledgment — a portion of the pattern that John has been tracking. Let him notice the closure and know what it means.
The MESA Operator
Bystander: Official (motivation: to be in control)

Not a villain. A professional assigned to manage an exposure situation. They are good at this. They have done it before. John is unusual to them — not because he's right (other people have been right) but because he's right in a way that connects to things that are usually separate. They find this interesting. They will not let "interesting" override "necessary."

  • Provide an alternative explanation for the pattern that is sophisticated enough that John can't immediately dismiss it — which tells John something about who he's dealing with.
  • Escalate, if John forces the question — not to violence, but to a more active form of management that John will feel but won't be able to prove.
