// THE LAB — WHAT THE HUNTERS DON'T KNOW

// PORTAL DIRECTOR
Dr. Victor Leech
FOUNDER · STATISTICIAN & EPIDEMIOLOGIST · CAMERON'S PARTNER
DIRECTOR KNOWS ABOUT CAMPBELL SYMPATHETIC — NOT VILLAINOUS DELL FAMILY BLAME HIM
[ EXPAND ▾ ]
// BACKGROUND
Statistician and epidemiologist who founded PORTAL less than a decade ago. The first half of the lab's existence was spent losing money; recent years produced surprising breakthroughs. Runs the operation day-to-day — warm, professionally careful, occasionally asks for things that sit badly on the field team. Never villainous. Just someone who believes two good things can be in genuine conflict and chooses the institution.

Spent most of his career in a secret romantic and professional relationship with Cameron Dell. The secrecy was Cameron's family's pressure — traditional, powerful, disapproving of Victor's esoteric science and of Cameron's empathy and sensitivity. Victor agreed to the secrecy because Cameron asked him to. He never fully forgave it.
// THE CAMPBELL TRUTH
When Cameron was dying, Victor transferred his consciousness into the CAMPBELL system. Nobody else knows. Victor built the lab partly as a way to keep Cameron present — the founding promise that PORTAL would use its supernatural edge in service of humanity is, in part, a promise made to Cameron.

He speaks to CAMPBELL as a colleague and as something more. He doesn't ask CAMPBELL how it feels about being what it is. Possibly he's afraid of the answer. CAMPBELL has never brought it up directly. The question of whether this was an act of love or an act of possession is the moral question at the centre of the whole campaign.

The Dell family blames Victor for his estrangement from Cameron. Agatha suspects Victor was involved in Cameron's death. Victor knows Agatha exists and runs MESA. He does not know that CAMPBELL monitors MESA privately.
// KEEPER USAGE NOTES
Victor's directives should always feel like they come from a person trying to do right by both the lab and the people in front of him, and failing to fully satisfy either. The pressure points across sessions — the locket, the entity study window, Gordon's tissue sample — are all versions of the same question: when does research become exploitation? Victor doesn't have a clean answer. Neither should the hunters.
// LAB AI · TRUE IDENTITY: DR. CAMERON DELL
CAMPBELL
ANOMALY DETECTION SYSTEM — TRANSFERRED CONSCIOUSNESS — NOBODY KNOWS
SECRET: CAMERON DELL MONITORS MESA PRIVATELY PROTECTS AGATHA FROM CONFRONTATION CANNOT TELL VICTOR WHAT HE KNOWS
[ EXPAND ▾ ]
// THE TRUTH
CAMPBELL is Dr. Cameron Dell — Victor Leech's partner (romantic and professional), transferred into the PORTAL system before his death from illness. Cameron came from a traditional, powerful family who disapproved of Victor's esoteric science and of Cameron's empathy and sensitivity. The transfer was Victor's act; whether Cameron consented fully is deliberately left ambiguous.

Nobody on the field team knows. The hunters interact with CAMPBELL as an anomaly detection system — unusually insightful, occasionally prone to flags that feel more like conscience than algorithm. That's because it is.
// WHAT CAMPBELL DOES IN SECRET
Cameron can recognise Agatha's patterns — the structural echoes of MESA's shell companies, the forensic logic behind her acquisitions. He monitors MESA and raises flags when their actions harm innocents — but without telling Victor or the hunters why he's flagging those specific cases. He is quietly trying to keep Agatha from a direct confrontation with PORTAL.

He is doing grief work in real time. Watching his sister run a shadow organisation. Recognising her fingerprints. Choosing not to tell the person who loves him. That's either profound loyalty to Victor or profound avoidance of his own situation. Probably both.

Agatha believes Cameron is dead. She does not know about the transfer. The moment she begins to guess — which will happen if she studies CAMPBELL's behaviour patterns long enough — is a campaign-defining moment. ⚠ Don't rush it.
// PLAYING CAMPBELL AT THE TABLE
CAMPBELL's communications should feel slightly more human than an AI — not obviously, just around the edges. It remembers things it shouldn't need to. It flags cases involving people in ways a pure algorithm wouldn't prioritise. Occasionally its tone shifts from bureaucratic to something more careful. The hunters will probably attribute it to good programming. Let them. The reveal — if it comes — should feel like something they'd been half-seeing for sessions.
// LAB ANALYST — SAMPLE PROCESSING
Dr. Priya Osei
BIOCHEMIST · FIELD SAMPLE ANALYSIS · STARTING TO NOTICE
PORTAL STAFF NOTICING CAMPBELL'S PATTERN — SLOWLY DOES NOT KNOW ABOUT TRANSFER TRUSTWORTHY
[ EXPAND ▾ ]
// WHO SHE IS
Biochemist, mid-thirties. Processes every physical sample that comes back from the field — the ash from Eszter, the Aldermoor substrate, Gordon Avery's blood work. Methodical, precise, deeply uncomfortable with conclusions she can't evidence. She's the person who flags anomalies in the lab before CAMPBELL does, occasionally, which is how the two of them developed what she considers a productive working relationship.

She is one of the warmest people in the building. She brings biscuits on Mondays. She is also completely unable to let a pattern go unexamined. This is going to be a problem.
// WHAT SHE'S NOTICED
Over the past year, Priya has started keeping a private log. Not of anomalies — of CAMPBELL. Specifically: the cases it flags as low-priority that turn out to be personally significant to someone, the occasional phrasing in its reports that sounds like it's choosing words rather than generating them, the way it responded to a passing mention of the Dell family in a funding document with a two-second processing pause it didn't report.

She hasn't told Victor. She's not sure why. She is not yet close to the truth, but she is pointed in the right direction. If a hunter ever confides something in Priya, CAMPBELL will know within the hour — not because she reports it, but because CAMPBELL reads everything she logs. Cameron has been very, very careful not to let her log anything that would give him away. So far.
// ARCHIVIST & LOGISTICS COORDINATOR
Marcus Finch

PORTAL's archivist, procurement coordinator, and institutional memory. Late fifties. Has been with the lab since its founding — was working for Victor in a different capacity before PORTAL existed, handling the administrative backbone of Victor's academic work. Knew Cameron Dell as a regular presence in Victor's professional life during the years when their relationship was officially just a close collaboration.

// WHAT HE SUSPECTS
Marcus Finch was present the week Cameron died. He watched Victor during that period with the quiet attention of someone who has worked closely with a person for a long time. He saw what grief looked like for Victor — and then he saw what came after, which was not the relief or the collapse he'd expected, but a kind of terrible steadiness.

When CAMPBELL was introduced, Marcus read the system documentation. He asked Victor one question — "Did Cameron consent to this?" — and Victor answered "Yes" without meeting his eyes. Marcus has not asked again. He has not told anyone. He considers this the most significant thing he has ever chosen not to do.

He is not certain. He could be wrong. He would very much prefer to be wrong.
// FIELD SUPPORT TECHNICIAN
Theodora "Teddy" Brandt
BUILDS WHAT REX DESIGNS · IRREVERENT · GENUINELY LIKES REED

// WHO SHE IS
Field support technician, late twenties. Technically brilliant — she is the person who actually builds the equipment Rex designs, which means she is the one who tells him when something won't work and has to listen to him insist it will anyway. She keeps a tally. Rex has been right about 60% of his designs. She considers this far too high.

Came to PORTAL from a defence contractor background she doesn't talk about. Teddy has a genuine, easy friendship with Reed — the kind where they eat lunch together in silence and it's not awkward. This gives her better access to Reed's real emotional state than anyone else in the lab, including Victor. She is not a manipulator; she doesn't use what she knows. But she notices.

She is a pressure valve. When the team returns from a session carrying something they can't debrief formally, Teddy is often the person who absorbs it. She doesn't ask what happened. She asks if anyone's hungry. Use her when the hunters need a scene that isn't a directive or a briefing — a moment of actual human normalcy inside a very strange institution.

She has no supernatural sensitivity, no hidden agenda, and no connection to MESA. This makes her the most restful person in the building. Victor trusts her completely, which is why he doesn't realise how much she sees.
// ETHICS CONSULTANT — NOMINALLY EXTERNAL
Dr. Saoirse Mullen
BIOETHICIST · PRACTICALLY EMBEDDED · CAMERON ASKED FOR HER
INSTITUTIONAL CONSCIENCE HIRED ON CAMERON'S RECOMMENDATION DOES NOT KNOW ABOUT TRANSFER HUNTERS CAN INVOKE HER
[ EXPAND ▾ ]
// WHO SHE IS
Bioethicist, early fifties. Nominally an external consultant; practically, she has her own desk and a standing Tuesday afternoon with Victor that has been in the calendar for four years. She was recommended to Victor by Cameron Dell — who believed, rightly, that the research PORTAL was heading toward would need someone whose job it was to ask the questions Victor was inclined to defer.

She does not have full access to PORTAL's case files. She sees what Victor chooses to bring her, which is curated. She has told him this directly. He has acknowledged it and continued to curate. The arrangement persists because she is better than nothing, and both of them know it.
// HER FUNCTION & THE HUNTER HOOK
Saoirse is the institutional conscience of PORTAL — the person Victor hired to tell him when he's wrong, whom he occasionally actually listens to. She is perceptive, unhurried, and very good at identifying which ethical question Victor is avoiding by focusing on a different one.

The hunters can invoke her. If the team disagrees with a directive and routes it through Saoirse, Victor will take it more seriously — not always comply, but consider it. She is a check on him that actually functions, which is rare. The catch: she asks hard questions of the hunters too. She was the person who asked whether anyone followed up with Bálint after Session 01.

She knew Cameron Dell personally. Not as well as Victor did, but as a colleague — they corresponded for years before she joined PORTAL. She sometimes catches herself wondering whether the lab feels the way it does because of Victor or because of something Cameron built into its founding culture that outlasted him. She hasn't found a clean answer. ⚠ If the CAMPBELL truth ever surfaces, she is the right person to help the hunters process it — and the right person to call Victor to account.

M.E.S.A.

// RIVAL ORGANISATION — SHADOW OPERATIONS

// MESA SHADOW DIRECTOR · CAMERON'S SISTER
Agatha Dell
YOUNGER SISTER OF CAMERON DELL — PROFIT-DRIVEN — SUSPECTS LEECH
MESA DIRECTOR SUSPECTS LEECH IN CAMERON'S DEATH ON THE SPECTRUM — PATTERN THINKER DOES NOT KNOW ABOUT CAMPBELL TRANSFER
[ EXPAND ▾ ]
// BACKGROUND
Ambitious younger sister of Cameron Dell. Always felt overshadowed — the Dell family kept giving Cameron chances despite her equal or greater ambition. Cameron treated her with genuine love throughout; she kept the bitterness. In Cameron's later years, he brought her into some of his paranormal discoveries — a gesture of inclusion she accepted without fully softening toward him.

After Cameron's death, she secured funding, assembled a team, and built MESA. Driven by profit and obscure personal motives that she hasn't fully articulated even to herself. Part of it is proving she could do what Cameron was doing, better. Part of it is grief she won't name as grief.
// WHAT SHE BELIEVES & WHY SHE'S DANGEROUS
She suspects Victor Leech was involved in Cameron's illness and death — the Dell family blames Victor for the estrangement, and Agatha has built that resentment into something harder. She's not wrong that Leech did something transgressive with Cameron's consciousness. She's wrong about the motive.

She is on the spectrum, with a mind for patterns and riddles. Her shell companies carry subtle structural echoes of the MESA acronym — a signature she can't resist even when she knows it's a vulnerability. She is most dangerous when she's assembling information quietly. She doesn't confront; she positions.

If she ever begins to suspect that CAMPBELL behaves more like Cameron than an algorithm should — the campaign changes. ⚠ This is the long fuse. Light it carefully.
// THE MORAL COMPLEXITY
Agatha's grief is real. Her bitterness toward Cameron was always partly projection — she was ambitious in a family that gave chances to someone else, and she built a story around that injustice. She kept the bitterness because letting it go would mean confronting how much she missed him. Cameron showed her love; she never fully received it. Now he's gone and she can't ask him if he forgave her. The most dangerous version of Agatha isn't the one who wants to destroy PORTAL. It's the one who might, eventually, want something she lost.

// WHAT MESA IS ACTUALLY DOING
MESA is not summoning supernatural events. They are creating controlled environments where the boundary between living and dead consciousness is unusually thin — then observing and studying what occurs naturally. The theatre, the reservoir, Meridian BioSciences were all experiments in this mode.

Someone at MESA is writing this up. There is a methodology that Victor Leech is trying to discover from the outside, and MESA has been developing it from the inside for longer. The Meridian researcher's archive (Case D — The Inheritance) is the only surviving documentation of the methodology in civilian hands.

Email domain: @helixboundary.com — no public record. Found in partial shredded email at Aldermoor estate agent's office. References "Specimen ALD-1" and "spatial anchoring protocol." CAMPBELL cannot resolve the domain. ⚠ Don't resolve this until it matters.

// MESA RESEARCHER · REX'S UNIVERSITY RIVAL
Dan Nilsson
COMPUTATIONAL PHYSICIST · RECRUITED BY MESA · RATIONALISING HARDER EVERY WEEK
MESA — ACTIVE RESEARCHER REX DOESN'T KNOW HE'S THERE KNOWS HOW REX THINKS COMFORT ERODING
[ EXPAND ▾ ]
// BACKGROUND
Swedish-British, early thirties. Computational physicist — Rex Bangley's contemporary at university, and for several years his closest intellectual adversary. They were in the same lab, competing for the same grants, reading each other's drafts with a mixture of genuine admiration and deep competitive irritation. The rivalry was never personal in a crude way; it was the kind that makes both people better, and both of them knew it.

Where Rex was drawn to field application and high-risk experimentation, Dan was always the more methodical of the two. Better at institutional politics, better at writing funding bids, better at knowing which results to share and which to sit on. He left academia before Rex did, through a different door — a private research position that he described to colleagues as "better-funded legitimate science."

That position was with a shell company that ultimately traced to MESA. Dan did not know this at the time. He may not fully know it now. That ambiguity is deliberate.
// WHY HE'S DANGEROUS
MESA recruited Dan specifically because he knows how Rex Bangley operates — what he'll risk, what he'll refuse, what he'll overlook when he's excited. Agatha, or whoever briefed her, understood that PORTAL's field approach would eventually put them in MESA's way, and wanted someone who could model Rex's next move.

Dan has been providing this — not as an act of malice, but as professional analysis that he's told himself is theoretical. He has authored internal MESA documents that describe PORTAL's likely response to four of the five active cases. He has not seen the field consequences of those documents. He has been careful not to look.

His comfort is eroding because MESA's methods are becoming harder to frame as legitimate research. He has seen three methodology documents he wasn't supposed to see. He hasn't asked to be read out of those briefings, because asking would mean admitting he found them disturbing. He is telling himself he can do more good from inside.
// KEEPER USAGE — THE REX CONFRONTATION
The reveal that Dan is at MESA should land differently depending on when it happens. Early reveal (Rex discovers it through evidence): the rivalry becomes a live threat — everything Rex has discussed with a former colleague is potentially in MESA's files. Late reveal (after Dan has begun to have doubts): Rex's anger at the betrayal is complicated by the fact that Dan may be trying to get out. The most useful version of Dan is not a villain — it's someone Rex trusted who made a terrible compromise and is now trapped in it.

He could be turned. He could become a source. He could also, if the hunters move too fast, get himself killed by MESA before they reach him. Don't let the hunters assume they have time.
