The Shōjō

A Monster of the Week Mystery
Situation Preparation for the Keeper
// WORKING TITLE — UPDATE AFTER SESSION

How This Prep Is Organised

This is a situation, not a script. The doc gives you the world, the people in it, and what each of them wants — then it names the problems in front of the hunters and the things they might notice. It does not tell you what the players will do. When a player announces something, find the closest matching "If the hunters…" move and run with it, or improvise from the situation.

Difficulty shorthand: TRIVIAL no roll · EASY basic roll, success likely · MODERATE real roll, costs on miss · HARD bad miss hurts · DEADLY people can die

THE SITUATION — BACKGROUND

What's Happening

A Japanese sea spirit called a Shōjō is in the Inner Harbor, feeding on human emotion. It came over eight weeks ago with a ceremonial sake cask shipped by Hosokawa Shipping — a cask brewed in grief, which the spirit drank half-dry within a week of docking. It cannot leave the cask. It cannot stop drinking. When the sake runs low, it drinks from the next thing that holds feeling: people on the waterfront. Three have died. A fourth will die tonight if nothing changes.

The Casks — Six in the Shipment, One Cursed

The Hosokawa cultural exchange shipped six ceremonial casks to Baltimore eight weeks ago, all from breweries in Hyōgo Prefecture. Five are normal ceremonial sake — high-grade junmai daiginjō, expensive, ordinary. The sixth is the cursed cask: a traditional cedar taru, 60 litres, half-full, painted with the Tachibana brewery's mon. Visually, it does not stand out from the others. The casks share a wooden cradle row in Storage Hold A. Yuki cannot tell them apart by sight. Neither can anyone else who hasn't held the Tachibana letter and read it.

The cursed cask is a catalyst — same principle as the prompt book in S03. It summoned the Shōjō and is the only thing that can resolve the situation. Destroy it and the spirit is loose. Use it correctly and the spirit leaves.

The sake inside is the weapon and the currency. It can be coated on weapons (+1 harm vs the Shōjō), poured in a circle (containment boundary the creature cannot cross while someone in the circle is genuinely feeling something), or offered in a cup with real emotion (the Drink With the Spirit move).

Finding the Right Cask

The cask is hidden in plain sight. The team has to identify it before they can act on it.

  • Rex's scanner reads the cursed cask as BIM-active. The other five read as nothing. But the cursed cask's signature is muted during the day while the Shōjō is dormant — readable, but only at close range (within 1m, hands-on or nearly so). At dusk the signature spikes and becomes unmistakable.
  • The brewer's letter identifies the cask by name (Akira Tachibana, the brewery mon visible on the side). If the team gets Yuki to open the letter in Scene 1, they can match the mon to the cask in the hold.
  • Yuki herself might recognise it if shown evidence the cask is the threat — she'll do the cross-reference if asked, after she believes.

Yuki is protective of the casks during the day. She does not yet believe one is supernatural. They are her company's property, they are expensive (~$8,000 each), and she will not let strangers manhandle them on the strength of vibes. A glamour-as-federal might earn a *look* in Storage Hold A. It will not earn the right to scan or take. Yuki will want to be convinced — she is curious about strange things — but she will not be convinced by nothing.

This changes after dusk. When the Shōjō surfaces and the field activates, Yuki's professional caution evaporates. She believes. She helps.

KEEPER NOTE — Why The Cask Can't Just Be Taken in Scene 1

The cask is the engine of the session. If the team grabs it in five minutes, the session collapses. Three protections, in layered order:

  • Identification. They have to find which of six casks is the right one. Scanner needs close range. Letter needs Yuki's cooperation.
  • Access. Yuki controls the storage hold and is suspicious of strangers handling product. Glamour-as-federal gets a look, not a take.
  • Consequence. Even if they identify and grab the cask in Scene 1, see "The Shōjō Tracks the Cask" below — moving it surfaces the creature. They can't just drive away with it.

If players are clever and committed, they can still pull the cask before dusk. That's a legitimate path — see Daytime Cask Grab in Scene 1 for what that looks like. But the easy version (Alan glamours, walks in, takes it) doesn't survive Yuki's protectiveness OR the identification problem.

The cask is settled, until it isn't. Eight weeks in the climate-controlled hold, undisturbed, has put the cask into a stable feeding cycle — the Shōjō surfaces at dusk, drinks, returns to the hull. Disturbing the cask disturbs the spirit. See the Stirring Clock below: each disturbance ratchets up the creature's agitation going into the climax. Players still have every option to scan, move, open, or carry the cask. Each option just changes how the climax begins.

Stirring Clock — Keeper Tool

Tracks how agitated the Shōjō is going into the climax. Different from the Encounter Clock (which tracks the fight itself). The Stirring Clock fills based on what the team does with the cask before the confrontation.

Fills (+1 segment) when:

  • The cask is physically moved more than ~5m from its cradle
  • The cask is opened (lid removed, sake drawn off)
  • A hunter handles it invasively — hands-on scanning, prying, lifting
  • Sake from the cursed cask is consumed by anyone outside ritual offering
  • Someone tries to damage or restrain it (lead-line a container, paint over the mon, etc.)
  • The cask is removed from the barge entirely

Does NOT fill for:

  • Sitting near the cask, talking near it, looking at it
  • Yuki's normal handling — she has been doing this for weeks without trouble
  • Rex scanning at distance >1m
  • The brewer's letter being read aloud nearby (the cask recognises this and goes quieter, briefly)
0/4 — DORMANT Default state. The Shōjō surfaces at dusk regardless. Standard climax — Encounter Clock starts at 1/4. Armour 2 (or 1 with letter understood). The team controls the tempo. 1/4 — STIRRING The Shōjō surfaces 30-45 minutes early. Mariana sees a shape in the water before the team is ready. Field activates earlier than expected. Encounter Clock starts at 1/4 but the team has less prep time. 2/4 — AWAKE The Shōjō surfaces immediately on disturbance, regardless of time. Aggressive from the first round — opens with Staggering Blow rather than singing. Encounter Clock starts at 2/4. The diplomatic window narrows. 3/4 — HUNTING The Shōjō tracks the cask actively. It pursues whoever moved it last, ignores other emotional sources to do so. It can move further from the cask than usual (anywhere on the dockyard, not just within 80m). Encounter Clock starts at 3/4. Drink With the Spirit is at +0 (no penalty, but no advantage either — the creature is past listening easily). 4/4 — LOOSE The bond between cask and spirit is torn ragged. The Shōjō can move anywhere within Baltimore. It begins to remember between segments — it remembers who hurt it. Climax armour is 3, not 2. Encounter Clock starts at 3/4. Drink With the Spirit requires +1 forward to attempt, and on a 7-9 the creature ignores the offering entirely (treat as miss). The team is now fighting an enraged sea god, not a grieving one.

The Stirring Clock is a budget the team spends. Identifying the cask costs 1 (close-range scan or hands-on inspection). Moving it costs 1. Drawing off sake costs 1. A patient team that scans from distance, gets Yuki's cooperation, lets her handle the cask, and only moves it once at dusk arrives at the climax with the clock at 1/4 or 2/4. An aggressive team that grabs and runs at 4:30pm can hit 3/4 or 4/4 before the Shōjō even arrives. Both are playable. The keeper's job is to make the cost visible (the cask hums louder, the fog tightens, distant singing) so the players can feel themselves spending the budget.

The Shōjō Tracks the Cask

Bound to the cask, not the location. Wherever the cask goes, the Shōjō follows. Distance does not break the bond.

  • Move the cask to the Suburban: the Shōjō surfaces and walks toward it (Stirring Clock +1).
  • Drive the Suburban out of the harbour: the Shōjō walks toward the city, slow but inexorable. Civilians on the route start dying. (Stirring +1 if they leave the harbour proper.)
  • Try to leave Baltimore: the field comes with the cask. The team is now a contagion. People they don't know start dying in towns they pass through.
  • Hide the cask in a sealed container, lead-lined room, etc.: the bond is BIM, not olfactory or visual. The Shōjō still tracks. Containment slows the approach but does not stop it. (Stirring +1 — the cask reads the attempt as restraint.)

Where the Shōjō Is During the Day

Submerged. Asleep against the hull of the Hosokawa barge, in the cold harbour water near the keel. Not visible from the surface. A diver would see something they couldn't unsee.

It surfaces at dusk by default — the cask's pull weakens as the light fails (the brewer wrote his "darkest hours" line for a reason; the sake was meant to be drunk in lit rooms with company, and the field activates when neither condition is met). Surfacing takes about five minutes — visible from the eastern container alleys as a slow shape rising in the fog.

It also surfaces if the cask is moved, regardless of time. Disturbing the bond wakes it. Daytime grab = daytime confrontation. The team can theoretically bait this — provoke the Shōjō to surface in daylight when there are fewer civilians, more witnesses, faster PD response. Tactically aggressive. Witnesses are a real problem.

The Residuals — Three Hot-Spots, One Entity

The Shōjō leaves a faint charge wherever it has fed. The charge fades over days. All three death sites are residual hot-spots, fading echoes of the same atypical signature. Rex's scanner will confirm: one entity, three deaths. This is the team's confirmation that they are dealing with a single supernatural threat, not three coincidences.

  • Rusty's, the third stool — five days old, weakest. A faint warmth on the seat. Theresa avoids it without knowing why. Scanner: weak echo, fading.
  • Pier 5, Eleanor's bench — three days old, stronger because Eleanor's death was longer and quieter. The bench wants you to stay. Scanner: medium echo, distinct.
  • The Hosokawa loading bay, between D-7 and D-8 — one day old, strongest. The spot where Pete died. The fog pools here even in the afternoon. Scanner: strong echo, the closest match to the cask's signature.

The three echoes share the same atypical class. Cross-referencing them with the cask reading gives Rex the click moment: this is one entity, anchored to the cask, leaving traces wherever it feeds.

MESA's Play

MESA modelled the cask's signature six weeks ago and has been observing remotely since. A grief-cask anchored to a sea spirit is a natural-source BIM artefact — something they have never had. They want it. Tonight is when they move.

They also did three things in advance:

  1. Accelerated CAMPBELL's attention. The deaths are real. The acceleration is theirs. They fed the pattern through channels CAMPBELL watches — forensic bulletins, Baltimore PD signal traffic, coroner database flags — so the case surfaced this Friday rather than next Tuesday. They moved the alarm clock to coincide with their other operations.
  2. Primed the mob. Fed intelligence (real grievance figures, internal Hosokawa hiring data) to a small group of dockworkers whose anti-Hosokawa anger was known and exploitable. The mob that forms at dusk is half-organic and half-manufactured.
  3. Pulled PORTAL apart. Sven was assigned earlier today to support a secondary deployment (see Sven below). The Director resisted; CAMPBELL flagged the case as imminent and the Director relented. The full field team is now split across two locations on a Friday night.

Rook is not in Baltimore. She is at one of the other active threads. The four grunts she sent want the cask intact and will hurt people to get it.

The Hosokawa Programme

Hosokawa Shipping runs a cultural exchange programme out of a converted barge at the Inner Harbor. Corporate receptions, diplomatic tastings, press visits. Three years operating. The programme has displaced some local longshoreman labour through a preferred-vendor hiring chain. The grievance is real. It's been simmering for a year. Tonight it catches fire.

THE THREE VICTIMS

Pulaski has the manila folder. Each victim has a witness statement, a coroner's report, a crime-scene photograph, and a location the team can visit. The team will ask about all three; let them choose which to chase first.

The Hosokawa Barge

A converted tourist barge, repainted in Hosokawa colours, moored at the easternmost private mooring on the quay. Storage Hold A — climate-controlled, accessed through a service corridor behind the reception tent. The hold is locked from the outside; Yuki has the key. Without the key it's MODERATE to break into during normal hours, EASY during the chaos of dusk. The reception tent: visible from the mooring approach. Catered, decorated. A tasting station near the entrance is where Yuki will offer hunters a sample if they board before 6pm.

The Longshoremen's Break-Shed

A converted shipping container at the western end of the Hosokawa quay, set back from the cranes. Folding chairs, a card table, a battered fridge, a TV that mostly shows static. The Drunk Question

A hunter who drinks the cask's sake takes: +1 ongoing Use Magic and Investigate (perception more permeable), −1 ongoing Act Under Pressure and Kick Some Ass (drunk), +1 harm taken from Emotional Drain (more permeable). One or two hunters drunk is a tradeoff. Whole team drunk is a party wipe.
