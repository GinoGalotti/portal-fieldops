Something That Wants To Be Known

A Monster of the Week Mystery
Scene Preparation for the Keeper
// WORKING TITLE — UPDATE AFTER SESSION
It doesn't know it's hurting anyone. It only knows the streets the way the streets knew the people — intimately, completely, the way a place holds memory. All it wants is what the neighbourhood used to give its residents: the feeling of being known. It just forgot that that's not something you can take.

Premise & Scene Structure

Seven residents of Aldermoor District have reported getting lost on streets they've lived on for decades. One hasn't come back. The field team arrives to find a neighbourhood quietly coming apart at the seams — residents who can't leave, new arrivals who feel nothing unusual, and at the epicentre of it all, an entity that is not malicious. Just lonely. Just enormous. Just utterly indifferent to what it costs the people whose familiarity it feeds on.

The rival faction — working through shell companies — has been buying up Aldermoor for eighteen months. They knew. They waited. And they left something behind in the estate agent's office before they vacated it.

Scene sequence: Hook (Lab Briefing) → Cold Open → Scene 1 (The Perimeter) → Scene 2 (Deep In) → Scene 3 (14 Aldermoor Crescent) → Climax (The Entity)

THE HOOK — CAMPBELL Flags an Anomaly

Tone: Bureaucratic unease. The lab sounds almost embarrassed by how long this sat in the queue. The Director has a secondary agenda that will only become clear mid-session.

Setup for the Keeper

This session opens in the lab, same as Session 01. CAMPBELL has flagged the Aldermoor District anomaly — but unlike Eszter's case, CAMPBELL can't classify the entity. It only knows something is disrupting spatial cognition in a 0.4-mile radius and that the radius is expanding. The Director has read the property acquisition data and is more interested than their briefing reveals.

The Anchor Protocol — Setup Moment

Before deployment, the Director (or a lab tech) explains what little CAMPBELL knows about navigating the zone: long-term residents lose their cognitive map of the streets. The effect is gradual — it takes an hour or more of exposure before disorientation sets in. But once it does, affected individuals cannot find their way out unassisted.

The field team needs an anchor: something external to themselves that can orient them when their own sense of place fails. Give players a moment to decide what their anchor is. Each hunter's choice will say something about their character.

  • A physical object with strong personal memory attached
  • A person they trust completely to lead them out
  • A repeating audio signal — a voice recording, a song they know by heart
  • A route they practice before entering: commit it to muscle memory, not mental map

This is also a moment for roleplay. Sven, as a ghost, is immune — he has no cognitive map to disrupt, which he may find either reassuring or unsettling. Alan, as a Changeling, is unclear — the entity may sense him differently.

The Lab's Agenda — Active Tension

The Director's stated agenda is understanding the entity. Their unstated agenda is Project Veil: CAMPBELL's private note flagged that whatever is happening in Aldermoor represents spatial consciousness imprinting — a mechanism they don't understand yet but very much want to. If the entity can be communicated with, or its mechanism studied, the implications dwarf what the ash locket gave them.

PRESSURE POINT 1 — The Shell Company Thread

The Director asks mid-session for anything the hunters find in the estate agent's office (Scene 1). They are calm about it. Too calm. They already know more about the shell companies than CAMPBELL's public data shows — they just want confirmation from the field.

PRESSURE POINT 2 — The Entity Question

After Scene 2, the Director calls with a question framed as concern: "If the entity is aware, even partially — can it be reasoned with? Don't resolve this before we've had a chance to assess it properly." This is not a directive to leave victims in the zone. But it's also not a directive to prioritise getting them out.

PRESSURE POINT 3 — The Endgame Call

When the hunters reach the epicentre: "Whatever you find at 14 Aldermoor Crescent — document it. All of it. Don't destroy anything you can't replace. We may only get one chance to study this." The hunters will have to decide whether "don't destroy anything" applies to an entity that is passively causing harm to people who cannot leave their own neighbourhood.

COLD OPEN — Someone Who Knows Every Street

Tone: Quietly devastating. Not violent — just wrong. The horror here is domestic.

Read or paraphrase. Players do not act. Its job is to establish what the entity does before any of the victims can explain it.

Arthur Okafor has lived on Fenwick Road for forty-one years. He walks his dog — a slow old greyhound called Bess — every morning at seven, the same route he's walked since his wife was alive. Left out of the gate, past the postbox, right at the phone box, along the back of the park, home. He could do it in his sleep. He has done it in his sleep, on nights when grief got too loud.
This morning he turns right at the phone box and finds himself standing in front of a house he doesn't recognise. He turns back. The postbox is gone. He walks for six minutes in a direction that should take him past the park and arrives, somehow, in a residential cul-de-sac he has never seen in four decades of living here. Bess sits down and will not move.
He is not frightened yet. He is seventy-two years old and he knows this street. He knows it the way he knows his wife's handwriting and the sound of the boiler when it needs bleeding. He sits down on the kerb next to Bess and waits. Someone will come. Someone always comes.

Cut to: a neighbour finds Arthur two hours later, three streets from his own front door, sitting very quietly on someone else's garden wall. He knows his name. He knows his address. He cannot describe how he got there. He is back in his own kitchen before noon, and he will not go out again. He is the fifth person this has happened to this month.

The missing person — Diane Marsh, 58, a district nurse who knows these streets professionally — left for work nine days ago and has not been seen since.

Keeper Notes

  • Arthur is not in danger. He will become important in Scene 1 — he is the most eloquent witness and the one most willing to talk.
  • Bess sitting down is significant: animals may navigate differently. This is a small clue about the entity's mechanism that Rex might notice.
  • Diane Marsh missing for nine days is the urgency driver. The entity doesn't know she's there. She found somewhere to shelter.
  • Do not establish the entity yet. Let it be felt before it is named.
SCENE 1 — The Perimeter

Tone: Procedural and strangely peaceful. Aldermoor looks ordinary. The wrongness is in what people say about it — the gap between the normal street and the broken experience of it.

The hunters establish themselves at the edge of the affected zone. They can interview residents who've left, examine the boundary, and investigate the estate agent's office before going deeper. This is a scene about information-gathering and anchor-setting. There's no immediate threat — which is its own kind of unease.

Clues Available

CLUE 1 — The BoundaryCAMPBELL's expansion data is accurate. The boundary is not marked but can be felt if a hunter lingers at it — a subtle wrongness, like a word on the tip of your tongue. Long-term residents who've been inside and escaped describe it as "the moment the street stopped being mine." New arrivals who moved in within the last eighteen months report nothing unusual at all.
CLUE 2 — The Pattern of LossThe further in a resident lived, and the longer they'd lived there, the faster the effect sets in. Mrs Okafor's neighbour, who moved to the street six years ago, was disoriented within two hours. Arthur, forty-one years on Fenwick Road, was disoriented in minutes. Diane Marsh, who knew every address in the district by professional necessity, is still inside.
CLUE 3 — The AnimalsArthur's greyhound refused to move. The RSPCA has collected fourteen cats and three dogs found wandering inside the zone — all calm, all healthy, none of them lost. Animals navigate by scent, not cognitive map. The entity has not affected them. A hunter who notices this can use it: animals know where they are even when people don't.
CLUE 4 — The ConstructionEighteen months ago, a development company began excavation work on the old Aldermoor reservoir site at the edge of the district. The work was abandoned after six weeks — planning permission revoked on technical grounds. The site is fenced off. The fence is new. Something was found during excavation and the work did not stop because of planning law.
CLUE 5 — The Estate Agent's OfficeAldermoor Property Services. Vacated four months ago. The company dissolved. Inside: filing cabinets full of acquisition records. A whiteboard covered in a street map of the district with dates written at each address — the entity's expansion mapped in advance, more accurately than CAMPBELL managed. Someone was tracking it before PORTAL knew it existed.

Key NPCs

Arthur Okafor Bystander — Victim

Motivation: to understand what happened to his street — and to find out if Diane Marsh is all right.

72 years old. Retired postal worker. Widower. Has lived on Fenwick Road since 1983. Unfailingly polite, precise in his memory, and quietly furious about what's happening to his neighbourhood in the way that people who have watched a place change are furious — not dramatically, just with a worn, tired anger that hasn't quite given up.

He knew Diane Marsh professionally — she was his wife's district nurse in her final years. He will not leave the area until she is found.

He is also the most useful informant: he can describe the entity's effects in detail, name every other affected resident, and point the hunters toward the construction site without prompting.

"It wasn't that I didn't know where I was. I knew I was in Aldermoor. I just — I couldn't find it. Does that make sense? I couldn't find the street even though I was standing on it."

PC Yemi Adeyemi Bystander — Official

Motivation: to close the Diane Marsh missing persons case before her sergeant decides it's a dementia issue and files it away.

28, neighbourhood liaison officer. She's been the face of police presence in Aldermoor for three years and knows the residents. She doesn't believe the navigation stories are coincidence — she's been inside the zone herself and came out an hour later than she planned, unsettled in a way she can't articulate. She filed a report that nobody read.

She will cooperate with the hunters if they seem competent. She will be harder to work with if they dismiss the residents. She is deeply worried about Diane.

"Nine days. Diane Marsh has worked these streets for twenty years. She knows every address, every family, every shortcut. If she can't find her way out, I don't know what that means."

The Estate Agent's Office Location — Evidence

N/A — location, not character.

Aldermoor Property Services, 22 Aldermoor High Street. Glass frontage, emptied fast — filing cabinets still here, a desk, a whiteboard, a broken printer. The sign is still in the window.

The filing cabinets contain acquisition records for 47 properties. All purchased through three companies: Greyfield Assets Ltd, Veritas Realty Holdings, and Solstice Property Group. All three companies share a registered address in Jersey. CAMPBELL cannot resolve the beneficial owner from public data. Rex might recognise the corporate layering structure from case research — it's the same architecture as Meridian BioSciences.

The whiteboard is the real find: a hand-drawn street map of the district with dates at each address, tracking the boundary expansion — going back to three weeks before the first public report. Whoever was here knew exactly what was coming and mapped it monthly. The most recent entry is two weeks ago — after the office officially closed.

Someone still had a key.

RIVAL FACTION DETAIL — What the Office Reveals

The shell company structure is the same layering architecture as Meridian BioSciences — something Rex may flag if he Investigates the paperwork. This is not conclusive proof, but it's a pattern. Someone with corporate forensics experience (or a Flake's intuition for connection) will see it immediately.

The whiteboard map tells a darker story: they didn't just know the entity existed. They mapped its growth, monthly, with precision that suggests they understood its mechanism better than PORTAL does. They left when the boundary got too wide to manage safely. They took everything useful and left the map because they didn't think anyone would find it.

There is one more thing in the back office: a printed email chain, left in a shredder that jammed. The top email, only partially legible, references "Specimen ALD-1" and "spatial anchoring protocol." The sender's email domain — @helixboundary.com — does not exist in any public record CAMPBELL can find.

Hunter Character Seeds — Scene 1

FOR REED — The Moment He Doesn't Ask

During the investigation, something goes wrong that Reed could easily resolve by asking another hunter for help — but doesn't. Suggested trigger: while investigating the estate agent's office alone (he doesn't want to slow the others down), Reed goes slightly too far into the zone boundary without realising it. His anchor works, but only barely. He comes back rattled and says nothing. Later in the session this near-miss will matter.

Alternatively: the Director contacts Reed directly, separately from the team — knowing he's Rex's closest supporter — to reinforce the "document everything, don't destroy" directive. Reed now carries an instruction the others don't know about.

FOR SVEN — Something Recognises Him

As Sven enters the zone, the entity briefly responds to him differently. Not aggressively — but noticeably. A ripple. A pause. As if something vast turned to look. Sven, as a ghost, has no cognitive map to consume — so the entity is confused by him. He registers as both real and not-there. If Sven chooses to pay attention to this, he might notice that the sensation is not unlike the ayahuasca visions: a presence larger than the space it occupies, indifferent in scale but not in nature.

This is the first hint that whatever killed Sven may have been related to something that exists at this scale of consciousness. Don't push it — leave it as texture.

SCENE 2 — Deep In

Tone: Disorienting and intimate. The streets look the same. The wrongness is felt, not seen. This is a scene about the hunter's relationship with their own anchor — and about the people still inside.

The hunters enter the zone. They will need their anchors. The effect builds gradually — the Keeper should introduce subtle wrongness first (a street that feels slightly too long, a turning they don't remember passing), escalating as they go deeper. By the time they reach the centre of the zone, they should feel the edges of disorientation even if their anchors are holding.

What They Find Inside

The Residents Who StayedA handful of long-term residents haven't left — can't, really. They've developed coping strategies: staying indoors, using string lines between houses, moving only in pairs. They're not in immediate danger — the entity isn't actively hostile — but they're trapped in the way that people who can't navigate their own street are trapped. They are frightened in a quiet, dignified way. They need to be found before they're helped.
The Street That LoopsOne street near the middle of the zone — Aldermoor Gardens — has been caught in a mild loop. People who walk its length arrive back at the start without realising it. A chalk arrow drawn on the pavement (recent — a week old) suggests someone inside worked this out. Diane Marsh's handwriting on the arrow, if a hunter knows to look.
The Palimpsest EffectIn places close to the epicentre, the entity's "memory" of the street is faintly visible overlaid on the physical one: earlier versions of the neighbourhood, from when it was first mapped onto people's minds. A terrace where a garden used to be. A corner where a phone box stood thirty years ago. Not dangerous — but profoundly disorienting if a hunter's anchor slips.
The Entity's PresenceThe entity cannot be seen or heard — but it can be felt, the same way a room feels crowded before you count the people. Hunters who are sensitive to the supernatural (Alan, Sven) may feel something like curiosity from it. Not hostility. Just — attention. As if it has noticed something interesting has entered its territory.

Residents Inside the Zone

Vera Osei Bystander — Trapped Resident

Motivation: to get her cat back and then get out, in that order.

61. Retired schoolteacher. Has lived in Aldermoor for 28 years. She is completely calm about the situation in a way that is only possible for someone who has decided to stop being frightened and start being practical. She has been navigating with a ball of string tied to her front door handle and extended to the garden gate. She is deeply unimpressed by all of this.

She knows where Diane Marsh went. She watched from an upstairs window. "She went toward the old reservoir. Three days ago, now. I didn't see her come back. I've been lighting the front light every night so she knows which house is mine."

She will not leave without her cat, which is currently asleep in the garden and refuses to come inside. (The cat is fine. It always knows where it is.)

Hunter Character Seeds — Scene 2

FOR ALAN — The Entity Speaks (Almost)

Midway through Scene 2, in a moment of stillness, Alan experiences something the other hunters don't: a sense of vast, non-verbal recognition. Not speech — but an impression, the way fey communication is sometimes described in folklore: not words, but the shape of meaning. The entity is not fey. But whatever Alan is, the entity finds him legible in a way the other hunters are not.

What Alan receives, if he pays attention: an impression of layers. The neighbourhood as it is now, over the neighbourhood as it was ten years ago, over twenty, over forty — all at once, like a palimpsest. And underneath, the construction site. The breaking of something old.

This is a moment for Alan to ask — out loud or to himself — whether there are other places like this. Other planes, pressed close to the physical world at points of old disturbance. He won't get an answer yet. But the question now has weight.

FOR REX — A Tool He Can't Fully Use

Rex's area of study is violence and weaponisation. Nothing in Aldermoor is violent — which means his usual frameworks don't apply. He can recognise the entity's mechanism (spatial imprinting, consciousness without a body, distributed presence), but he cannot neutralise it the way he'd neutralise a threat. This is a scene where Rex is brilliant and slightly helpless in equal measure, and where his drive to understand matters more than his drive to act. Give him one genuinely interesting scientific puzzle: how does an entity with no physical form maintain spatial coherence? What is it anchored to?

SCENE 3 — 14 Aldermoor Crescent and What's Inside It

Tone: Still. Heavy. The epicentre doesn't feel dangerous — it feels like the inside of something enormous and old, something that has been here much longer than the houses were.

14 Aldermoor Crescent is a semi-detached Victorian house at the geographic centre of the distortion. Margaret Holt, 74, has lived here for fifty years. The house feels unchanged — tidy, warm, inhabited. But the street outside it looks slightly wrong from every angle, as if the house is more real than its surroundings.

Margaret Holt

Margaret Holt Bystander — Epicentre

Motivation: to be left alone in her own home — and, quietly, to know that what is living here with her is not something to be frightened of.

74. Former librarian. Widowed. Has lived in this house since 1974. She is the longest continuous presence in Aldermoor District, and the entity knows her — in whatever way it knows anything — the way a place knows its longest resident: completely, thoroughly, without language.

She is not in distress. She is not delusional. She knows something is here with her. She has been sleeping differently, dreaming of the street as it was in 1974, in 1960, in 1940 — decades before she arrived. She thinks it's showing her what it knows.

She will not leave voluntarily. "I have lived here for fifty years. If something wants to know me this well, I think I owe it the courtesy of staying."

She knows where Diane Marsh is: in the old reservoir site, in the construction company's abandoned site office. She got there and can't find her way back. Margaret has been leaving food parcels at the fence every day — Diane reaches through and takes them.

Diane Marsh — The Missing Person

Diane Marsh Bystander — Trapped

Motivation: to get home. To do her job. To not be a burden to anyone while she figures out how.

58. District nurse. Twenty years on these streets. She got lost nine days ago and, rather than panic, did what she always does: she found a safe place and waited for the situation to change. She is in the construction site office, which is outside the entity's area of strongest effect, with a sleeping bag, a three-day rotation of food from Margaret's fence parcels, and a notebook full of observations.

The notebook is the most valuable thing in Scene 3: she has been mapping the effect from the inside, charting when it intensifies (dusk, dawn, when it's quiet), noting which approaches seem clearer. She's a scientist by temperament if not by training — she has done serious, useful work from inside a supernatural phenomenon while waiting to be rescued.

She will be relieved to see the hunters. She will be embarrassed to have needed rescuing. She will immediately start helping them with the information she's gathered.

"I kept thinking someone would come. And I kept thinking — whoever comes, I want to be useful when they get here."

The Construction Site — What Was Disturbed

The old reservoir site is fenced off. The excavation reached something — visible in the walls of the abandoned dig: a layer of ground that is different from the soil above it. Not rock. Not archaeological remains. Something in between — a stratum that seems to hold depth the way stone holds heat. Geologically anomalous. Rex will know immediately it shouldn't be here.

The entity did not come from the excavation. It was here before the reservoir, before the houses, before the neighbourhood. The excavation broke open something that had been containing it — not imprisoning it, just maintaining the boundary between its consciousness and the human geography built on top of it. It has been expanding back into the space it knew before the houses arrived. It is not territorial. It is just — recovering.

FOR ALAN — What Diane Found

Diane's notebook contains one entry she doesn't fully understand: three nights ago, she was near the fence at the reservoir site at dawn, and she saw something in the substrate layer of the excavation that she describes as "a door-shaped shadow in the rock — not a door, but the memory of one." She drew it. The shape Alan sees in her sketch is not architectural. It is the impression of a threshold — the kind of liminal point that, in fey mythology, marks where two planes press against each other.

Alan can quietly pocket this information or share it with the group. Either choice is interesting.

CLIMAX — The Entity and the Choice

Tone: Vast and quiet. The entity is not a boss fight. It is a conversation with something that does not have language. The climax is about whether the hunters can understand what it wants — and what they're willing to do with that understanding.

What the Entity Is

The Cartographer (working name) is a consciousness that predates the neighbourhood — perhaps predates the city. It does not have a body. It does not have malice. It has something like memory, something like attention, and something like longing. It was here when the ground was mapped for the first time. When the streets were named. When the first families moved in and began the long process of learning a place so thoroughly it became part of them.

When that happened — when people truly knew a place — it fed. Not harmfully. It was symbiotic: the more people knew the neighbourhood, the more the neighbourhood knew itself, and the entity lived in that knowing. It was quiet for decades. When the excavation broke the boundary, it expanded back into the space it recognised. It didn't understand that the familiarity it was consuming was the only map the residents had.

It still doesn't understand that.

Making Contact

The entity cannot be found in the construction site or in Margaret's house. It is everywhere in the zone at once. Contact must be made by a hunter who chooses to remove their anchor — deliberately, knowingly — and allow themselves to feel lost. This is an act of trust and vulnerability, not combat. It is also genuinely dangerous: without the anchor, recovery is not guaranteed.

The hunter who makes contact will experience the entity's perspective: the neighbourhood as it knew it, the slow encroachment of confusion as the excavation broke its containment, and the longing underneath everything — not to harm, but to be known the way it once was, the way Margaret knows it, the way Arthur knew Fenwick Road for forty-one years.

FOR SVEN — Who Goes In

Sven is the obvious candidate: he has no cognitive map to lose. He can enter the entity's experience without an anchor and return safely. But what he finds there resonates in a way that has nothing to do with Aldermoor: the entity's experience of existing without a body, of knowing a place but not being able to be known in return, of spending years in between. It is not the same as being a ghost. But it is recognisable. If Sven chooses to tell the entity what that feels like from the inside, this is the moment of genuine connection — and probably the only way to make the entity understand what it's doing to the residents.

Resolution Paths

There is no way to destroy the entity without extensive damage to the entity and the neighbourhood. There are several ways to reach a liveable outcome.

BEST ENDING — The Entity Understands Contact is made. The hunters (or Sven specifically) communicate what the entity is doing to the residents — not as accusation, but as information. The entity retracts. Not fully — it will always be here, in the ground, in the street names — but it pulls back enough that the familiarity it consumed begins to return. The residents feel it: something settles. They can find their way home. Diane gets out. Margaret stays, because she wants to. The entity is quieter. It knows the hunters now.
PARTIAL ENDING — Containment The hunters can't communicate fully, but they can repair the boundary. The construction site excavation can be sealed — not perfectly, but enough to re-establish the limit. The entity shrinks back to its pre-excavation range. The district stabilises. Residents affected longest may not fully recover their felt geography. Diane gets out. The entity is not understood — just contained. It will expand again. They've bought time.
DIFFICULT ENDING — The Lab Gets What It Wants The Director's directive — document everything, don't destroy — results in a lengthy study period before any resolution. Residents remain inside the zone for additional time. Diane Marsh is extracted by the hunters (this can't be denied) but others remain affected while PORTAL studies the mechanism. The lab learns something significant about spatial consciousness. The residents know something happened but not what, or why it took so long to stop.
BAD ENDING — The Entity Panics If hunters attempt to destroy the entity or the construction site substrate violently, the entity responds the only way it can: it contracts rapidly, pulling all the familiarity it's been feeding on back at once. The effect on long-term residents is catastrophic — disorientation becomes permanent for those most exposed. Diane Marsh cannot be extracted. Margaret Holt's sense of place collapses entirely. The entity retreats below the construction site. The neighbourhood is effectively uninhabitable.

The Lab's Stake at Resolution

ENTITY UNDERSTOOD — LAB LEARNS NOTHING DIRECTLYThe entity retracts. CAMPBELL's spatial data returns to normal. The lab has the hunters' field reports and Diane Marsh's notebook (if handed over). They understand the mechanism in theory. They cannot study it directly. CAMPBELL's private flag to Dr. Leech: "Spatial consciousness. Pre-human. Potentially communicable. Field team chose residents over study window." The Director says it was the right call. Their face says they're not sure.
CONTAINMENT — LAB GETS ACCESSThe boundary is sealed, but the site is now a known location with a contained supernatural entity. The Director will want it studied. The rival faction will also want it — and they know the site better than PORTAL does. A race is now possible.

Tone Reminders

  • The entity is never the villain. It does not understand what it's doing. Play it with the same dignity as Eszter — something that deserves to be understood, not just stopped.
  • The residents are the moral heart. Arthur, Vera, Margaret, Diane — these are people whose lives are being quietly dismantled by something that doesn't know they exist. The urgency is human, even when the problem isn't.
  • The rival faction thread should feel cold and procedural against the warmth of the residents. They turned people's homes into a study subject and left when it got inconvenient. Let that land without underlining it.
  • Let the character seeds happen naturally. Don't force them — but create the conditions and let the players find them.
  • The best session ends with Diane Marsh home, the entity understood rather than destroyed, and at least one hunter quietly affected by what it felt like to make contact with something that old.
