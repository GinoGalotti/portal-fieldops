HOSOKAWA QUAY · OFFICE TRAILER · M. COSTA, NIGHT SHIFT
WRITTEN 21 OCT 2005 · AT THE REQUEST OF DET. R. PULASKI

I have told this story four times. This is the fifth. I am writing it down because Pulaski asked me to and because writing makes me less angry than telling.

Thursday, 23:15. I was in the office trailer doing manifest checks. Pete was on the radio with Frank arguing about the Tachibana shipment. They were screaming. Then they went quiet. Quiet is worse than loud on a night shift.

I went around D-7 to look. Pete and Frank were holding each other and crying. I have known these men four years. I have never seen either of them cry. They were holding each other like brothers. It was the most beautiful thing I have seen on a dock and I hated it.

Past them, between D-7 and D-8, in the fog — there was someone. Tall. Taller than anyone. Red. Not a costume red, not a face-paint red. Old red. Brick that has been weathered for a hundred years.

It was swaying. Very slightly. And it was singing. Low voice, words I didn't know, in a language I have heard at the docks but slower and lower, like it came from underneath.

It looked at me. I want to write that down because I haven't said it out loud. It looked at me. Not threatening. Like it noticed me, decided I wasn't what it was looking for, and went back to what it was doing. Pete fell down. Frank kept holding him. The thing kept singing.

I called dispatch. I have not been back to D-7.

I have seen it twice before. Monday between D-3 and D-4 — I thought I was tired. Wednesday by the eastern fence near the barge mooring — only for a second. Then Thursday. Each time it was further east. Each time it was closer to the barge.

— SKETCH (M. COSTA, BALLPOINT ON LEGAL PAD) —
— what I saw, between D-7 and D-8
