Clara Voss — Rehearsal Notebooks

Selected entries, spanning eighteen months. Three notebooks recovered from Clara's flat via Miriam Okafor.

RECOVERED: Bedside drawer, Ward B, Spotsylvania Regional Medical Center
ORIGINAL LOCATION: Clara Voss's flat — sister had custody, unaware of contents
RECOVERED BY: Reed Atwood, during interview with Miriam Okafor
NOTEBOOKS: 3 (Moleskine, black, A5). Entries span 18 months.
CONDITION: Good. Later entries show heavier pen pressure. Final entry incomplete.
Notebook 1 — Week 2 of Season 1

First full run of Act II with Edmund. His Proteus is quieter than I expected — he's finding the betrayal through stillness rather than agitation. It works. I adjusted the Silvia entrance to give him an extra beat before my line. Petra noticed and approved without saying anything, which is how you know it was right.

The prompt book arrived for the anniversary display. Marcus is very excited about it. Beautiful object — Victorian, annotated throughout in a hand I can't quite read. He let me hold it during the blocking session. Heavier than it looks.

// KEEPER: Clara's baseline voice. Professional, specific, observational. CLUE: first physical contact with the prompt book — incidental, she doesn't know what it is yet.
Notebook 1 — Week 8 of Season 1

Good week. The Act V blocking is finally settled — we landed on playing Proteus's assault as full remorse rather than menace. It's more disturbing this way. Valentine's forgiveness reads as horrifying instead of noble, which I think is correct. Edmund agrees. Marcus is nervous about it. Petra told him it was the right choice, which settled it.

I've been using the prompt book as a reference during blocking notes — the Victorian annotations have a logic to them I'm starting to understand. Not stage directions exactly. Something about the relationship between the performer and the space. Whoever wrote these was thinking about performance differently from the way we do.

// KEEPER: Still fully Clara. CLUE: "the relationship between the performer and the space" — she's reading the mechanism's instructions without knowing what they are.
Notebook 1 — Week 3 of Season 2

Second season opened well. The Silvia choices are more settled now — I don't have to think about the entrances, they just happen. Edmund said my instincts have never been sharper. I said his haven't either. We meant it as a compliment both ways.

Something odd in Thursday's matinée. I knew my next line before the cue. Not because I'd memorised the sequence — I've been off-book for a year. This was different. It felt like remembering something that happened rather than something I learned.

Probably just deep familiarity with the role. Three seasons of the same lines in the same space. The body learns before the mind catches up.

// KEEPER: First symptom. CLUE: "remembering something that happened" — the earliest sign of role-anchored transfer. Clara rationalises it reasonably.

Notebook 2 — Week 11 of Season 2

It's happening more. Making choices in rehearsal that I don't remember deciding. The blocking adjustments are good — Petra marks them without comment, which means they're working — but I can't always trace them back to a thought I had. It's as if the decision happens in the space between intending to move and moving.

Edmund noticed. He said something about my choices having "a quality of inevitability" that he finds remarkable. He meant it as a compliment. I smiled and said thank you.

I did not say: I don't know where the choices are coming from.

// KEEPER: Clara can't name it but documents it precisely. CLUE: decisions arriving without her making them — the mechanism operating below conscious awareness.
Notebook 2 — Week 14 of Season 2

I should tell Edmund. I keep not telling Edmund.

I don't know how to say: I think something is happening to me that I don't have a name for, and it feels like excellence, which is the part that frightens me most.

Thursday's performance was the best Silvia I've ever given. I could feel the audience — not in the usual way, not the awareness of four hundred people watching, but something more specific. As if I knew what they needed from the scene before they did. As if the scene was already complete and I was arriving inside it.

I held the prompt book for twenty minutes during the notes session afterward. I don't remember picking it up.

// KEEPER: "It feels like excellence" — the line the players should carry. CLUE: audience connection describes the BIM amplifier. CLUE: prompt book contact now habitual and unconscious.
Notebook 2 — Week 6 of Season 3

Silvia does not like the way Valentine speaks to her in Act III. This is not an interpretation. It is not a character note. I am writing it down because the feeling arrived complete, fully formed, without me thinking it.

Silvia does not like the way Valentine speaks to her, and when I am onstage in that scene, I adjust my position to create more distance, and Petra has noted this as a deliberate choice, and I have accepted her reading because I don't know how to explain the alternative.

The alternative is that the choice was not mine.

Notebook 3 — Week 10 of Season 3

The touring production briefing is in two weeks. I have been planning this for two years — my Verona, my staging, the approach to Act V that I've carried since the first season. I want to see it through.

Yesterday during the Act II run I felt something settle behind my eyes like a second attention. Not intrusive. Patient. Waiting for the scene to require it.

Edmund was across the stage and I looked at him and knew — not thought, knew — that he would turn upstage on his next line. He did. He always does. I have watched him do it for three years. But the knowing was different. It came from inside the scene rather than from beside it.

// KEEPER: "A second attention" — Clara names the mechanism. CLUE: the knowing comes from inside the performance. Voice is shifting — "I have watched him do it" has a formality that isn't Clara's register.
Notebook 3 — Week 13 of Season 3

There are two of us in every performance now.

I do not mean this as metaphor. In the Act IV entrance — the one where Silvia refuses Proteus — I can feel the refusal arriving before I enact it. It is not my refusal. It belongs to someone who has refused this particular man in this particular way for longer than I have been alive. I step into the shape of it. The audience sees Silvia. They have always seen Silvia. What has changed is that Silvia is also watching.

I have not told Edmund. I will not tell Edmund. He is carrying something similar and I can see it in the way his Proteus has stopped being a performance and become a habitation, and if I name it for him the naming will make it real in a way it is not yet real, and I am not prepared to be responsible for that.

// KEEPER: No longer fully Clara's voice. CLUE: "There are two of us" — explicit. CLUE: "longer than I have been alive" — Silvia predates Clara. CLUE: Edmund is also inhabited — "a habitation."
Notebook 3 — Week 15 of Season 3

The staircase is unfamiliar at night. I have climbed it many times but tonight the steps do not correspond to the ones I remember. The upper rehearsal room is dark. The building is quiet. I am alone in it, which is true of me more often now than it used to be.

Edmund left after the double bill. He looked tired in a way I recognise — the tiredness of someone who is present inside something that requires more presence than they have. I wanted to tell him. I did not.

The touring production briefing is Thursday. I want to see it through.

I want to see it through.

// KEEPER: Clara's final entry, the night she died. CLUE: "The staircase is unfamiliar" — for two seconds she was Silvia, and Silvia has never climbed those stairs.
Notebook 3 — Final page, undated

She was planning something beautiful. I have seen it from inside her and it would have been extraordinary.

The stairs were dark. She knew them. I did not.
